# The Economic Impacts of Staffing Numbers: Nurses on Hospital Financial Results

This repository contains a research term paper analyzing the relationship between nurse staffing levels and the financial performance of hospitals.

**Course:** American Healthcare
**Semester:** [Spring 2025]
**Author:** [Lalit Madhav Viyyapu]

## Paper Overview

This paper examines the intricate relationship between nurse staffing levels and hospital financial outcomes[cite: 6, 14]. It argues that while nurse staffing costs represent a significant portion of hospital operating expenses[cite: 9, 10], understaffing can lead to much larger long-term costs due to increased patient complications, longer hospital stays, higher readmission rates, and increased nurse turnover[cite: 7, 12, 109].

The paper explores:

* The high direct and indirect costs associated with nurse turnover, including recruitment, training, lost productivity, and decreased staff morale[cite: 42, 51, 64, 110].
* The financial benefits of appropriate nurse staffing, such as improved patient outcomes, reduced errors and hospital-acquired conditions, shorter lengths of stay, lower readmission rates, and increased patient satisfaction, which can lead to significant cost savings and improved revenue[cite: 68, 74, 80, 111].
* Strategies hospitals can employ to optimize nurse staffing and financial performance, including investing in retention programs, implementing creative recruitment tactics, and utilizing flexible staffing models and technology[cite: 85, 93, 101, 113].

The research synthesizes findings from existing literature to highlight that the initial expense of hiring adequate nursing staff is often offset, and even exceeded, by the cost savings and revenue improvements resulting from better patient care and operational efficiency[cite: 7, 39, 120].
